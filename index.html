How to play 29

29 is a trick-taking game for 4 players in two partnerships. You and the top player (Partner) play against West and East. Play goes clockwise.

Cards and points

32 cards: 7 to Ace in each suit. In every suit the order from high to low is J, 9, A, 10, K, Q, 8, 7.

CardJ9A10K Q 8 7
Points32110

That makes 28 points in the deck. The winner of the last trick gets 1 extra point, which makes 29 in total.

Bidding

  • Each player gets 4 cards. Bidding starts with the player left of the dealer and goes clockwise.
  • A bid is the number of points your side promises to win, from 16 to 28. The first three players in turn may open or pass (the dealer may not open).
  • After an opening bid, the next opposing player answers, and the two sides bargain back and forth. On your turn you can raise, say ditto (match the current bid and take it over), or pass. Only the player who was holding the bid and has just been outbid can say ditto. Anyone else, including a partner who takes over, and anyone answering the opening bid or another ditto, must bid higher or pass.
  • A pass is final for that player, but then their partner takes over for the team. When both partners of a side have passed, the other side wins the bidding.
  • The highest bidder secretly picks a trump suit from the cards they hold. The other 4 cards are then dealt to everyone.
  • If everyone passes, the cards are redealt.

Playing

  • You must follow the suit that was led if you can.
  • Trump stays hidden. If you can't follow suit, you may press Show trump. The bidder reveals the suit, and you must play a trump card if you hold one. A higher trump wins over any other suit.
  • The bidder can also play a trump card when they can't follow suit, which reveals the trump automatically.
  • After trump is revealed, a player who can't follow suit may play any card.
  • The highest card of the led suit wins the trick, unless it was cut by a trump. The winner leads next.

Marriage (pair)

  • A player who holds both the King and Queen of trump in hand can declare a marriage once trump has been revealed, from the trick after the one where it was revealed. Both cards must still be in hand.
  • The game declares it for you automatically at the start of your turn when it's allowed.
  • If the bidding side holds the marriage, their target drops by 4 (never below 16). If the defenders hold it, the target rises by 4 (never above 28).
  • It doesn't apply in a single hand, since there is no trump.

Double and redouble

  • After the bidder chooses trump and before the second deal, each opponent of the bidder in turn may double if they think the bid will fail.
  • If a double is called, the bidder or the bidder's partner may redouble.
  • The stake for making or missing the bid is 1 game point normally, 2 if doubled, and 4 if redoubled.
  • If someone calls single hand, doubles no longer apply, because single hand has its own stake.

7th card rule

  • If you win the bid but don't want to pick a suit from your first 4 cards, choose 7th card.
  • In the second deal, the 7th card you receive is set aside face down. Its suit is trump, and only you can look at it.
  • Until trump is revealed, that card is not part of your hand: you can't lead it (unless it's your last card) and it doesn't count for following suit.
  • If you can't follow suit, you can show it and play it to declare trump. If someone else asks for trump, the card goes back into your hand.

Single hand rule

  • After the second deal and before the first lead, each player in turn (starting left of the dealer) may call single hand. The first player to call gets it, and it replaces the bid.
  • You can only call it if your hand is not a certain sweep (at least one card could lose a trick).
  • The caller plays alone against both opponents, with no trump, and leads first. The caller's partner sits out.
  • The caller must win all 8 tricks. Success: +3 game points. Failure: −3. The round ends as soon as the opponents win a trick.

Scoring

  • If the bidder's side wins at least as many points as the bid, it scores +1 game point. If it falls short, it scores −1.
  • The first side to reach +6 wins the game. A side that falls to −6 loses.
